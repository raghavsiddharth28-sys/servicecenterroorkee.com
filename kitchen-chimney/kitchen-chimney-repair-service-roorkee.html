Why Regular Chimney Cleaning & Service is Essential in Roorkee

Roorkee homes, family kitchens, restaurants, and cloud kitchens love authentic North Indian cooking. Daily meals involve deep frying, rich tadka, mustard oil, ghee, and roasted spices that release heavy oily vapors and carbon soot into the air. Over weeks and months of daily cooking, this sticky oil residue coats the baffle filters, builds up inside the blower housing, and sticks to the flexible exhaust duct pipe.

When filters choke and grease accumulates, air suction drops drastically, forcing smoke and irritating chili fumes to linger in your kitchen tiles and cabinets. Thick oil inside the motor housing creates a serious fire hazard around open gas flames and overheats the motor bearings. Regular servicing and professional deep cleaning ensure fresh kitchen air, quiet motor operation, lower power consumption, and long chimney life.

Common Kitchen Chimney Problems We Handle

Below are 30 common kitchen chimney problems faced by Roorkee homeowners, along with causes, technician checks, and solutions:

1. Chimney Not Turning ON
What You Notice: Complete loss of power; lights and motor do not respond to buttons.
Possible Reason: Blown PCB fuse, dead 16A wall socket, or severed internal power wiring.
What Technician Checks: Tests input mains voltage and checks PCB transformer output with a multimeter.
Possible Solution: Replace blown glass fuse, restore internal power terminals, or repair power cord.
2. Weak Suction (Smoke Lingering in Kitchen)
What You Notice: Motor runs, but smoke, steam, and chili odors fail to get extracted.
Possible Reason: Baffle filters choked with grease or aluminium duct pipe bent/compressed.
What Technician Checks: Measures airflow velocity at the filter face and inspects duct pipe bends.
Possible Solution: Deep chemical filter descaling (₹650-₹850) and straightening duct pipe.
3. Zero Suction Power
What You Notice: Blower fan motor runs on high gear, but no air draft is felt at the intake.
Possible Reason: Outer wall exhaust louver stuck shut with dirt or duct pipe disconnected.
What Technician Checks: Checks outdoor vent cowl and inspects duct connection at chimney top collar.
Possible Solution: Free external non-return valve flaps and re-clamp exhaust duct securely.
4. Excessive Smoke Coming Back into Kitchen
What You Notice: Smoke blows downwards from chimney edges back into your face.
Possible Reason: Duct pipe length exceeds 15 feet or non-return valve flap is jammed backwards.
What Technician Checks: Inspects back-draft damper and checks duct resistance against outdoor wind.
Possible Solution: Re-align non-return damper and reduce duct bends to prevent air backflow.
5. Chimney Making Excessive Rattling Noise
What You Notice: Loud metallic clatter or rattling whenever the chimney is operated.
Possible Reason: Loose blower lock nut, foreign object in fan, or loose chimney canopy screws.
What Technician Checks: Checks impeller tightness on motor shaft and inspects casing screw torque.
Possible Solution: Tighten motor shaft nut, balance blower wheel, and secure loose body panels.
6. Heavy Motor Humming or Buzzing Noise
What You Notice: Motor hums loudly without rotating or spins very sluggishly.
Possible Reason: Blown or weak motor capacitor, or seized motor shaft bearings.
What Technician Checks: Measures capacitance value with digital meter and tests manual shaft rotation.
Possible Solution: Replace faulty run capacitor (4µF/6µF) and lubricate shaft bearings.
7. Fan / Blower Impeller Not Rotating
What You Notice: Lights work, switches click, but fan stays completely still.
Possible Reason: Solidified grease locking the blower blades, or open motor winding.
What Technician Checks: Checks motor coil resistance (ohms) across speed taps.
Possible Solution: Degrease blower housing, free motor shaft, or rewind/replace burnt motor.
8. Motor Completely Dead or Burnt
What You Notice: Zero response from motor and slight burnt coil smell inside canopy.
Possible Reason: Motor overheated due to choked filters or prolonged electrical overload.
What Technician Checks: Tests winding continuity and checks for insulation breakdown to earth.
Possible Solution: Replace motor with exact wattage (180W-250W) copper winding unit.
9. Excessive Chimney Vibration on Wall
What You Notice: Whole chimney shakes, vibrating wall tiles and glass canopy.
Possible Reason: Grease accumulation on only one side of the fan, throwing balance off.
What Technician Checks: Inspects impeller blade symmetry and checks wall mounting anchors.
Possible Solution: Deep chemical wash of blower wheel to restore balance; re-anchor wall bracket.
10. Sticky Grease Buildup on Canopy Body
What You Notice: Yellowish thick oil coating stainless steel canopy, attract dust and flies.
Possible Reason: Long cooking intervals without wipe-down; saturated internal filters.
What Technician Checks: Evaluates grease depth and inspects internal housing seal.
Possible Solution: Complete chemical degreasing (₹850 deep clean) with food-grade solvents.
11. Heavy Oil Deposits Inside Blower Housing
What You Notice: Black grease dripping from motor bottom casing.
Possible Reason: Oil collector canal clogged, preventing oil from draining to the cup.
What Technician Checks: Disassembles motor shroud to check grease buildup volume.
Possible Solution: Industrial deep cleaning; scraping and dissolving internal grease sludge.
12. Baffle Filter Completely Blocked With Oil
What You Notice: Stainless steel baffle slats clogged solid; air suction completely blocked.
Possible Reason: Months of deep frying without soaking or cleaning filters.
What Technician Checks: Checks filter slat clearance and looks for corrosion or warped frames.
Possible Solution: High-temperature alkaline chemical bath descaling or replacement.
13. Thermal Auto-Clean Function Not Working
What You Notice: Auto-clean button pressed, but heating element does not warm up.
Possible Reason: Burnt heating element coil, failed thermal relay on PCB, or open sensor.
What Technician Checks: Tests heating element resistance and verifies PCB relay voltage.
Possible Solution: Replace auto-clean heating strip or repair PCB heating relay.
14. Touch Control Panel Not Responding
What You Notice: Touching the glass icons does not register or beep.
Possible Reason: Grease film over glass, loose ribbon cable, or defective capacitive touch IC.
What Technician Checks: Cleans glass underside and tests ribbon cable continuity to PCB.
Possible Solution: Clean capacitive touch sensors or replace the front touch membrane.
15. Push Buttons Jammed or Broken
What You Notice: Mechanical speed buttons stay stuck down or do not latch on speed 2/3.
Possible Reason: Hardened cooking grease gumming up the mechanical latch spring.
What Technician Checks: Tests mechanical latch mechanism and micro-switch contacts.
Possible Solution: Degrease switch bank or replace with new 5-key mechanical switch assembly.
16. PCB / Control Board Malfunction
What You Notice: Random speed fluctuations, erratic beeping, or controls failing to turn off.
Possible Reason: Voltage spikes damaging PCB micro-controller or humidity on solder traces.
What Technician Checks: Inspects PCB board for burnt copper tracks, swollen caps, or bad relays.
Possible Solution: Repair damaged solder tracks or replace with model-compatible PCB board.
17. Chimney LED Lights Flickering or Not Working
What You Notice: Cooking spotlights are completely dark or strobe constantly.
Possible Reason: Burnt 12V LED driver converter, loose wire spade, or blown LED chip.
What Technician Checks: Tests 12V DC output from the driver module with a multimeter.
Possible Solution: Replace internal 12V constant-current LED driver or LED spotlight unit.
18. Chimney Suddenly Switching OFF Mid-Cooking
What You Notice: Chimney stops running after 5-10 minutes and restarts only after cooling down.
Possible Reason: Motor internal thermal cutout (TOP) tripping due to grease friction and heat.
What Technician Checks: Measures operating motor surface temperature and checks bearing stiffness.
Possible Solution: Thorough deep cleaning, motor bearing lubrication, or capacitor change.
19. Slow Suction Speed on Highest Gear
What You Notice: Speed 3 runs at almost the same low speed as speed 1.
Possible Reason: Weak motor capacitor or burnt speed tap resistor on the PCB board.
What Technician Checks: Checks voltage delivery across individual speed tap terminals.
Possible Solution: Install fresh motor run capacitor or replace PCB motor speed control relay.
20. Burning Smell Coming from Chimney
What You Notice: Pungent burning plastic or wire smell inside the kitchen when running.
Possible Reason: Loose screw terminal overheating, burnt capacitor, or motor short.
What Technician Checks: Disassembles electrical box to inspect for melted plastic or charred wires.
Possible Solution: Trim damaged wire harness, fit ceramic wire connectors, and replace capacitor.
21. Internal Wiring Problem or Loose Joints
What You Notice: Chimney operates intermittently when the body is bumped or pressed.
Possible Reason: Loose spade terminals or wire vibration fatigue inside motor casing.
What Technician Checks: Checks wire continuity and crimp connector tightness.
Possible Solution: Re-crimp loose wire connectors and insulate harness with heat-shrink tubing.
22. Main Electrical MCB Tripping When Chimney Starts
What You Notice: Turning on chimney immediately trips the kitchen MCB breaker.
Possible Reason: Direct live-to-body electrical short or motor copper insulation burnt out.
What Technician Checks: Performs insulation resistance test using high-voltage megger/multimeter.
Possible Solution: Eliminate short circuit, isolate live terminals, or replace grounded motor.
23. Flexible Duct Pipe Blocked or Crushed
What You Notice: Motor roars on high speed, but smoke escapes from false ceiling or duct edges.
Possible Reason: Thin aluminium foil duct crushed behind cabinetry or clogged with thick grease.
What Technician Checks: Inspects duct path from chimney collar to outdoor wall hole.
Possible Solution: Replace damaged foil duct with heavy-gauge flexible aluminium duct pipe.
24. Loose Duct Connection Causing Smoke Escape
What You Notice: Smoke and oily smell seep out inside kitchen overhead cabinets.
Possible Reason: Duct clamp has slipped off the chimney exhaust collar.
What Technician Checks: Checks collar connection and seal tightness around exit hole.
Possible Solution: Re-mount duct with heavy-duty metal jubilee worm drive clamp and foil tape.
25. Outer Duct Louver Shutter Jammed Shut
What You Notice: Air cannot exhaust outdoors; backpressure makes chimney unusually loud.
Possible Reason: Outdoor plastic louver flaps glued together with oily dust or bird nest.
What Technician Checks: Inspects exterior wall exhaust cowl on outside building wall.
Possible Solution: Clean external louver flaps, remove obstruction, and ensure free flap movement.
26. Sticky Oil Dripping Down onto Stovetop
What You Notice: Black/brown oil drops fall into food while cooking on gas burners.
Possible Reason: Overflowing oil collector, saturated filters, or cracked collector tray.
What Technician Checks: Inspects internal drainage slopes and checks oil cup seal.
Possible Solution: Empty collector, deep clean internal drip canopy, and replace cracked cup.
27. Oil Collector Cup Leaking or Cracking
What You Notice: Oil seeps through collector cup base even when not completely full.
Possible Reason: Heat embrittlement cracking plastic oil cup, or loose locking tabs.
What Technician Checks: Inspects cup body under light for hairline cracks and checks latch fit.
Possible Solution: Install new heat-resistant polycarbonate or stainless steel oil collector cup.
28. Blower Fan Blade Cracked or Imbalanced
What You Notice: Severe motor wobbling accompanied by a rhythmic thumping sound.
Possible Reason: Plastic blower wheel cracked from heat stress or missing balance clip.
What Technician Checks: Inspects impeller fins for broken blades or hub cracks.
Possible Solution: Install brand-compatible replacement blower wheel with dynamic balance.
29. Loose Wall Mounting Brackets or Wobbling
What You Notice: Chimney body tilts forward or sways when motor starts up.
Possible Reason: Rawl plugs slipped out of brick/tile wall due to weight and vibration.
What Technician Checks: Tests wall bracket stability and verifies spirit level alignment.
Possible Solution: Re-drill and install heavy-duty metal anchor fasteners; level the unit securely.
30. Chimney Requiring Complete Deep Chemical Cleaning
What You Notice: Over 1 year since last service; air suction choked; grease on all seams.
Possible Reason: Continuous high-oil cooking causing heavy polymerization across all surfaces.
What Technician Checks: Inspects filter, blower, inner chamber, oil channels, and duct pipe.
Possible Solution: ₹850 full chemical deep cleaning; disassembly, degreasing, and suction test.
