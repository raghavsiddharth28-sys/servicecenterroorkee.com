Service vs Repair: What is the Difference?

Many customers wonder whether they need a routine service or a repair. Here is a simple comparison to help you choose what you need:

Washing Machine Service

Preventive maintenance to keep an operating machine in healthy condition.

  • Drum and tub descaling/cleaning
  • Cleaning inlet valve mesh and coin trap filters
  • Washing detergent dispenser drawer
  • Checking rubber door boot and hoses for early cracks
  • Testing water filling, drainage, and spin balance
  • Tightening loose belts and adjusting levelling feet

Washing Machine Repair

Troubleshooting and fixing a machine that is already broken or showing a fault.

  • Machine does not turn on or trips the main switch
  • Drum does not spin or makes grinding roaring noise
  • Water not filling or continuous overflow
  • Water not draining out (drain pump failure)
  • Display showing error codes (such as E18, 4C, 5E, dE)
  • Replacing damaged parts like PCB, motor, or door lock

What We Check During Washing Machine Service

During a routine doorstep service visit, the technician follows a thorough checklist to ensure all major systems are functioning properly:

Overall Machine Condition: Checking physical stability, levelling feet, and outer cabinet rust or damage.
Water Inlet System: Testing water intake speed, cleaning the sediment mesh filter, and inspecting the solenoid valve.
Drain System: Opening and cleaning the coin trap, checking the drain pump impeller, and flushing dirty water lines.
Lint Filters & Dispensers: Washing the lint collection bag or plastic cartridge and cleaning the detergent drawer.
Hoses & Connections: Checking both the inlet pipe and corrugated discharge pipe for kinks, leaks, or loose clamps.
Door & Rubber Seal: Inspecting the front load door boot gasket for mildew, cracks, and checking the door latch mechanism.
Drum & Spin Balance: Rotating the inner drum by hand to check for bearing friction, loose play, or scraping sounds.
Noise & Vibration Check: Running a test spin to verify that the shock absorbers and suspension rods damp vibration properly.
Basic Electrical Operation: Checking the main power cord, plug earthing, and looking for signs of wire overheating.
Full Cycle Test: Running a short wash, rinse, and spin cycle to verify water control and motor direction changes.

When Should You Get Your Washing Machine Serviced?

You do not need to wait until your washing machine stops completely. Schedule a service check if you notice any of these signs:

Unusual Noise:

Loud squeaking, rattling, or thumping sounds during wash or spin cycle.

Slow Drainage:

Water takes longer than usual to leave the drum, indicating filter blockage.

Low Water Flow:

Water fills very slowly into the tub, often caused by sand in the inlet mesh.

Foul Odor:

Musty or sour smell coming from the drum or washed clothes.

Excess Vibration:

The machine walks or shakes violently during high-speed spinning.

After Long Periods Without Use:

If the machine has been unused for months in a closed house or rental home.
