RO Water Purifier Technologies We Service

Modern water purifiers combine multiple sequential technologies to tackle physical dirt, dissolved salts, microbiological pathogens, and water taste. Understanding what each stage does helps you identify which component may need maintenance:

1. Reverse Osmosis (RO) Membrane

The core technology that forces pressurized water through a semi-permeable polyamide thin-film composite (TFC) sheet with pores measuring just 0.0001 microns. It rejects 90% to 95% of dissolved chemical salts (TDS), fluorides, nitrates, arsenic, and heavy metals, washing them out through the reject line.

2. Ultraviolet (UV) Disinfection

Water flows through a stainless steel chamber housing a high-intensity germicidal UV-C lamp (254 nm wavelength). The UV radiation penetrates microorganism cell walls, destroying the DNA of bacteria, viruses, and cysts. UV does not change water TDS or remove dissolved minerals.

3. Ultrafiltration (UF) Membrane

Utilizes microscopic hollow-fiber capillary tubes (pore size ~0.01 micron) that physically strain out suspended colloidal solids, cysts, and bacteria under natural tap pressure without requiring electricity or wasting reject water.

4. Multi-Stage Combinations (RO + UV, RO + UF, RO + UV + UF)

Depending on the purifier model, manufacturers combine these technologies so RO handles heavy dissolved solids while UV and UF provide redundant biological protection against microbial contamination.

5. TDS Controller / Modulator Valve

A precision manual needle valve that blends a small, controlled stream of pure micro-filtered UF water with RO permeate to adjust the final drinking water mineral level and taste.

6. Pre-Filter, Sediment & Activated Carbon Filtration

The 5-micron spun sediment filter traps raw sand, silt, and pipe rust. The activated carbon block adsorbs municipal chlorine, organic pesticides, and chemical odors to prevent chlorine from oxidizing the delicate RO membrane.

7. Post-Carbon, Mineralizer & Alkaline Cartridges

The post-carbon polishing stage removes any lingering odor from stored water. In selected models, an alkaline mineral cartridge re-introduces balanced calcium, magnesium, and potassium ions to maintain a pleasant, non-acidic pH.

8. Auto-Flush Solenoid & UV LED Stages

Model-specific features like automatic membrane flushing help sweep away concentrated brine crusts every time the machine starts, while UV LEDs in the storage tank keep stored water sanitized throughout the day.

Important: Not every water purifier contains all the technologies above. Features depend on your specific purifier model. We make no medical claims regarding water treatments; our service is focused on standard mechanical and electronic equipment maintenance.

Common RO Problems We Handle in Roorkee

RO water purifiers combine high water pressure, filtration chemistry, and low-voltage electronics. Below are 53 commonly encountered faults our visiting Roorkee technicians diagnose and resolve on-site:

#1

RO Not Turning On (Power Dead)

"RO bilkul on nahi ho raha, switch on karne par koi light ya sound nahi hai."

When an RO purifier does not show any life, the primary reason is usually an electrical interruption. This can be caused by a burnt SMPS power adapter, blown internal fuse, loose power cord, faulty wall socket, or a tripped high/low-pressure switch that cuts power to the circuit.

#2

RO Completely Dead (No Power to Pump or Lights)

"Machine poori tarah band hai, display aur indicator bhi off hain."

A completely dead RO unit often indicates an SMPS power supply failure or a burned-out PCB controller. Voltage fluctuations can damage the DC transformer. Our technician tests DC voltage output with a multimeter to verify power flow.

#3

RO Not Starting Even with Water & Electricity

"Light aur paani dono hone ke baad bhi RO start nahi ho raha."

If supply voltage and inlet water are available but the machine won't start, the Low Pressure Switch (LPS) may be stuck or malfunctioning, or the float switch in the storage tank may falsely signal that the tank is full.

#4

Water Not Coming Out of Tap

"RO ke tap se paani bilkul nahi aa raha hai."

No water flow from the dispensing faucet can result from an empty storage tank (due to non-purification), a choked post-carbon or mineral cartridge, a damaged dispensing tap, or an airlock inside the pressurized storage tank bladder.

#5

Very Slow Water Flow (Trickle Flow)

"Paani bahut dheere-dheere drop-by-drop ya patli stream me nikal raha hai."

Very slow dispensing flow is commonly caused by low tank air pressure in under-sink/pressurized tanks, sediment filter choking, scale buildup on the RO membrane, or a clogged inline carbon filter.

#6

Low Water Pressure Inside System

"System ke andar paani ka pressure kafi kam ban raha hai."

An RO membrane requires between 60 to 80 PSI to push water through microscopic pores. If the booster pump has a worn diaphragm or failing pump head, internal pressure drops and pure water generation nearly stops.

#7

RO Leaking Water from Base or Body

"RO ke niche se ya body ke andar se paani leak ho raha hai."

Water pooling beneath the RO unit typically stems from a loose elbow connector, cracked filter bowl, loose tube fitting, or a hairline fracture in the booster pump head or solenoid valve casing.

#8

Water Leaking from Pre-Filter Housing

"Bahari spun pre-filter cylinder se paani tapak raha hai."

The transparent or white external pre-filter housing uses an internal rubber O-ring seal. If the O-ring dries out, cracks, or gets misaligned during filter cleaning, water continuously drips from the housing threads.

#9

Water Leaking from Pipe / Push-Fit Connections

"Pipe ke joint ya push-fit elbow se paani spray ya leak kar raha hai."

Quarter-inch (1/4") or 3/8" food-grade RO tubes can degrade, get scored, or bend improperly at push-fit collar fittings. Replacing worn quick-connect elbows and re-cutting tubing ends creates a secure leak-free lock.

#10

Storage Tank Not Filling

"RO ka tank bhar nahi raha hai, ghanto chalne ke baad bhi khali rehta hai."

If the tank stays empty, the system might be dumping all incoming water through the reject pipe because of a scaled-up membrane, a stuck auto-flush valve, or a failed solenoid valve that refuses to open.

#11

RO Tank Filling Very Slowly (Taking 4+ Hours)

"Tank bharne me 4-5 ghante se zyada ka waqt lag raha hai."

Normal domestic RO tanks (7-10 liters) should fill within 45 to 90 minutes depending on membrane capacity (75 GPD / 100 GPD). Extended filling times indicate clogged pre-filters, high feed TDS, or low booster pump output pressure.

#12

RO Continuously Running (Never Stops)

"RO bina ruke lagatar chal raha hai, auto off nahi ho raha."

A purifier that never shuts off will overheat the pump and waste vast amounts of water. It usually points to a defective float sensor, a worn-out auto shut-off valve (ASV), or a ruptured storage tank bladder that cannot generate back-pressure.

#13

Auto Cut-Off Not Working (Tank Overflowing)

"Tank bharne par bhi cut-off nahi lagta aur paani bahar nikalta hai."

When water spills over from the clean tank, the mechanical micro-switch or magnetic float switch has failed to trigger the electrical relay, leaving the solenoid valve and pump energised.

#14

Booster Pump Not Stopping When Tank is Full

"Tank full hone ke baad bhi pump ki humming sound band nahi hoti."

A pump that stays running even when water dispensing has halted indicates a stuck High Pressure Switch (HPS) or a leaky check valve that constantly bleeds pressure back into the reject line.

#15

Booster Pump Making Loud Vibrating Noise

"Motor se bahut tezi se thak-thak ya khad-khad ki aawaz aa rahi hai."

Booster pumps vibrate excessively when internal ball bearings wear out, when air locks enter the pump head, or when mounting rubber vibration dampeners detach from the purifier chassis.

#16

Motor / Pump Not Working (Humming but Not Spinning)

"Pump sirf gunguna raha hai par paani aage nahi fek raha."

A humming motor that does not rotate may have a jammed diaphragm, severe mineral scale inside the pump head, or insufficient DC supply voltage from an ailing SMPS adapter.

#17

Bad or Bitter Taste in Purified Water

"Paani ka taste kadwa, ajeeb ya metallic lag raha hai."

Bitter or unnatural taste occurs when the post-carbon filter is exhausted and can no longer polish water, when TDS has dropped unnaturally low (acidic taste), or when mineral cartridges leach unbalanced minerals.

#18

Foul Smell / Bad Odor from Water

"Paani me se ajeeb si smell ya badboo aa rahi hai."

Odor in drinking water usually arises from stagnant water inside an uncleaned storage tank, expired activated carbon that has lost chlorine-adsorption capacity, or bacterial growth in output piping.

#19

Water Appears Cloudy or Milky White

"Glass me nikalne par paani safed ya dhundhla dikhta hai."

Cloudy water is often benign micro-bubbles caused by high water pressure through a fresh filter cartridge. If the cloudiness does not settle within 60 seconds, it may be due to suspended colloidal particles bypassing an exhausted sediment filter.

#20

General Water Quality & Purity Concerns

"Paani peene me halka ya fresh feel nahi ho raha."

When family members feel the water is too heavy or leaves a chalky residue on vessels after boiling, the membrane rejection efficiency may have deteriorated, requiring a calibrated TDS digital meter check.

#21

TDS Output Too High (Near Raw Water TDS)

"Pure water ka TDS kafi badh gaya hai, raw water ke barabar aa raha hai."

If input water is 500 ppm and pure water reads 350 ppm, the RO membrane is either ruptured, improperly seated in its housing, or the manual TDS controller valve is opened excessively.

#22

TDS Output Unusually Low (< 30 PPM)

"TDS bahut hi zyada kam ho gaya hai, paani bilkul flat lagta hai."

Unusually low TDS strips essential natural minerals, causing a sour or flat taste. Adjusting the TDS blending controller or installing an alkaline mineral cartridge restores healthy mineral equilibrium.

#23

TDS Controller / Blending Valve Problem

"TDS adjuster screw ghumaane par bhi TDS set nahi ho raha."

TDS controllers blend a tiny fraction of purified micro-filtered UF water with RO permeate. If the needle valve is jammed with salt deposits, TDS cannot be calibrated accurately.

#24

Complete RO Membrane Blockage

"RO membrane poori tarah jam ho chuki hai, paani aage pass nahi hota."

In areas with hard groundwater, calcium and magnesium carbonates crystalize across the polyamide thin-film composite (TFC) membrane sheets, choking the 0.0001-micron pores completely.

#25

Membrane Performance Degradation (High Salt Passage)

"Membrane purani ho gayi hai aur salt filter nahi kar pa rahi."

A membrane typically lasts 12 to 24 months depending on incoming water hardness and pre-filtration care. Over time, chemical wear reduces salt rejection below the acceptable 90%+ threshold.

#26

Sediment Filter Blocked with Sand & Rust

"Mitti aur ret se sediment filter poora bhar gaya hai."

The 5-micron spun polypropylene sediment filter captures raw silt, mud, algae, and pipe rust. When clogged, inlet water cannot reach the pump, starving the purifier of flow.

#27

Activated Pre-Carbon Filter Saturated / Blocked

"Carbon filter choke ho gaya hai aur chlorine smell nahi rok pa raha."

Granular or block activated carbon protects the sensitive RO membrane by neutralizing municipal chlorine and chemical contaminants. A saturated carbon block chokes water passage and allows chlorine to corrode the membrane.

#28

Post-Carbon Filter Exhaustion

"Post-carbon filter expire hone se paani me freshness nahi rehti."

Located between the storage tank and faucet, the post-carbon filter eliminates any lingering storage taste. When depleted, water may develop a stale or flat taste.

#29

UV Lamp / UV Chamber Failure

"UV chamber ki light nahi jal rahi ya continuous beep sound aa rahi hai."

Purifiers with ultraviolet disinfection beep an alarm if the 11W/8W UV lamp fuses or the UV ballast fails. Without active UV radiation, microbiological safety is compromised.

#30

UF (Ultrafiltration) Membrane Clogging

"UF filter jam hone se flow restrict ho gaya hai."

Hollow-fiber UF membranes (0.01 micron) remove cysts and bacteria without electricity. Silt accumulation within the hollow fibers restricts water flow, requiring thorough back-flushing or replacement.

#31

Purifier Making Whistling or Chattering Sound

"RO ke andar se seeti jaisi ya chattering sound aa rahi hai."

Rapid clicking or chattering sounds usually originate from an electrical Solenoid Valve (SV) fluttering due to unstable supply voltage or low water pressure fluctuating around the cut-in limit.

#32

Reject Water Continuously Flowing (Drain Never Shuts)

"Wastage pipe se lagatar paani behta rehta hai chahe tank bhar gaya ho."

Continuous reject drainage even when the purifier should be idle wastes hundreds of liters of water. It is almost always caused by a faulty Solenoid Valve (SV) that fails to seal shut, or a defective Auto Shut-Off Valve (ASV).

#33

Excessive Reject Water Ratio (Too Much Drain Flow)

"Reject water bahut zyada nikal raha hai par pure water ek boond nahi banta."

If the drain water is gushing heavily while pure water is nearly zero, the Flow Restrictor (FR) may have lost back-pressure resistance or the membrane is choked, diverting all water directly to drain.

#34

Very Little or Zero Reject Water Output

"Wastage pipe se paani bilkul nahi nikal raha."

A completely blocked reject line is dangerous for the RO membrane because concentrated brine cannot be flushed out, leading to immediate irreversible membrane scaling within hours. A choked Flow Restrictor (FR) is the typical cause.

#35

RO Draining Continuously When Switched Off

"Power switch band hone ke bawjood drain pipe se paani behta rehta hai."

If water flows to drain even with power turned off, incoming municipal line pressure is forcing its way through a mechanically jammed Solenoid Valve that cannot spring closed.

#36

Power Adapter (SMPS) Heating Excessively

"Power supply adapter bahut zyada garm ho raha hai ya jalne ki smell aa rahi hai."

An overheated SMPS adapter indicates severe overload, usually caused by an aging pump motor drawing excessive amperage (over 1.5A to 2A) or internal shorting in the secondary coils.

#37

SMPS Power Supply Voltage Drop Under Load

"Load padte hi SMPS voltage chhod deta hai aur pump band ho jata hai."

A failing SMPS may show 24V DC when idle, but as soon as the pump tries to start, the voltage collapses to 10V-14V DC, causing the pump to jerk and stall repeatedly.

#38

PCB / Electronic Control Board Malfunction

"Motherboard ya PCB chip kharab hone se purifier erratic behave karta hai."

Modern digital RO purifiers feature programmed logic boards controlling UV alarms, auto-flush solenoids, TDS displays, and filter life indicators. Moisture or power surges can damage PCB microchips.

#39

Water Sensor / Leakage Detection Sensor Trip

"Leakage sensor activate hone se buzzer baj raha hai aur RO band ho gaya."

High-end models incorporate bottom drip trays with electronic moisture sensors that cut off water supply and sound an alarm if internal seepage is detected.

#40

Solenoid Valve (SV) Jammed or Burned Out

"Solenoid valve khul nahi rahi ya band nahi ho rahi."

The 24V DC solenoid valve controls inlet water flow. Rust or scale can mechanically jam the plunger, or electric surges can burn the solenoid copper coil.

#41

Float Switch / Microswitch Misalignment

"Float switch atka hua hai jisse RO ko tank full hone ka pata nahi chalta."

The mechanical float inside the tank lifts with water level to press a microswitch. If the float arm gets jammed by scale or detaches, water will either not stop or never start.

#42

Pressurized Tank Air Valve / Bladder Failure

"Under-sink storage tank me air pressure khatam ho gaya hai."

Under-sink RO systems rely on a butyl bladder pressurized to 6-8 PSI with a Schrader air valve. When air bleeds out or the internal bladder tears, the tank feels heavy but water won't flow out.

#43

Internal Tubing Kinked or Pinched

"Machine ke andar pipe mud gaya hai jisse paani ruk gaya."

During filter replacement or servicing, stiff RO tubing can become sharply kinked inside tight purifier cabinets, severely constricting internal flow.

#44

Dispensing Tap / Faucet Leaking or Loose

"Nal / tap se lagatar boond-boond paani tapak raha hai."

The plastic push-tap or lever faucet on wall-mounted purifiers wears down through daily use. Internal silicone washers wear out, causing persistent dripping on kitchen countertops.

#45

Digital Display / Touch Panel Not Responding

"Screen par kuch show nahi ho raha ya touch button kaam nahi kar rahe."

Digital touch panels and LCD screens can stop responding due to flat cable corrosion, front-panel moisture ingress, or control board processor lockups.

#46

Indicator Lights Blinking Rapidly

"LED indicators fast blink kar rahe hain aur machine beep kar rahi hai."

Rapid blinking LEDs typically encode a diagnostic alert: continuous beep for UV failure, alternating red/blue for filter life expiry, or solid red for water starvation.

#47

Error Codes Displayed on Screen

"Screen par Error code jaise E1, E2, E3 aa raha hai."

Advanced purifiers show specific alphanumeric error codes denoting low inlet pressure, UV chamber malfunction, TDS variation, or leak sensor tripping.

#48

Filter-Change Warning Alarm Won't Reset

"Naya filter lagane ke baad bhi filter replacement alarm bajna band nahi hota."

Modern smart RO models have internal hour meters or flow counters. After changing filters, a specific hardware reset sequence on the front panel or PCB must be performed to clear the warning.

#49

Automatic Flush Valve Malfunction

"Auto flush valve open hi reh gaya hai jisse pressure nahi ban raha."

Auto-flush mechanisms purge the membrane surface for 15-30 seconds every time the RO starts. If the flush solenoid valve remains stuck open, high pressure never builds and no pure water is produced.

#50

Commercial RO Low Permeate Production

"Commercial plant ka water output drastically gir gaya hai."

Commercial 25 LPH, 50 LPH, or 250 LPH systems suffer reduced output when 4040/8040 commercial membranes experience biofouling, calcium scaling, or when high-pressure pump impellers wear down.

#51

Commercial RO Operating Pressure Imbalance

"Pressure gauge par reading galat dikha raha hai ya fluctuation hai."

Commercial systems operate at precise pressures (150-200 PSI for high pressure pumps). Fluctuation on panel pressure gauges points to cavitation, clogged micron cartridge filters, or multi-port valve bypass.

#52

Commercial RO Membrane Bio-Fouling & Scaling

"Heavy usage se commercial membrane par layer jam gayi hai."

Heavy continuous water processing in hotels, offices, and restaurants causes rapid silica, iron, or biological slime deposition on commercial membranes, requiring Chemical In-Place (CIP) cleaning or replacement.

#53

Commercial RO High-Pressure Pump Cavitation & Vibration

"Commercial vertical multistage pump aawaz aur vibration kar raha hai."

Commercial high-pressure pumps (such as CRI or Grundfos stages) cavitate when raw water feed pumps cannot deliver adequate volume through the multi-grade sand and carbon pre-treatment media vessels.

Crucial RO Purifier Safety Information

Water purifiers combine high electrical voltages with pressurized water lines. Please keep these essential safety practices in mind:

  • Electrical Precaution: Never attempt to open the rear electrical cabinet or touch SMPS transformer wires while the purifier is plugged into a wall outlet. Water droplets mixed with 220V AC wiring create severe shock hazards.
  • UV Radiation Warning: Never look directly at an illuminated Ultraviolet (UV) lamp. Germicidal UV-C light causes immediate, painful corneal burns and retinal damage. The lamp must only operate fully enclosed inside its stainless steel reactor chamber.
  • Pressurized Line Hazard: Booster pumps generate up to 80-120 PSI. Never disconnect quick-fit push fittings while the purifier is energized or while the raw water inlet ball valve is open. High-pressure water spray can cause electrical shorts and kitchen flooding.
  • Food-Grade Material Standard: Always ensure replacement tubing, filter bowls, and inline cartridges are certified food-grade and non-toxic to prevent harmful plastic chemical leaching into drinking water.
