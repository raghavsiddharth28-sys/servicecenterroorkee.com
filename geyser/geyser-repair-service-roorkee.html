1. Storage Geyser — Core Architecture

Storage geysers feature a heavy-gauge inner steel tank lined with vitreous enamel glass coating, polyurethane foam (PUF) insulation, 2kW or 3kW incoloy/copper immersion heating element, dual capillary thermostats (operating thermostat set at 65°C and manual safety cutout set at 90°C), sacrificial magnesium anode rod, and a multi-function safety pressure relief valve (PRV).

2. Common Storage Geyser Faults & Diagnostic Solutions

Fault: Water does not heat at all and indicator lamps are unlit

Root Cause: Heating element burned out or high-temperature safety thermal cutout tripped open.

Diagnostic Solution: Test 2kW heating element resistance (approx. 25–28 ohms), reset thermal cutout switch, and install new incoloy element if coil is open.

Fault: Water takes over 45 minutes to heat and feels only lukewarm

Root Cause: Heavy calcium carbonate sediment scaling around the heating element tube.

Diagnostic Solution: Open flange, remove calcified sediment crust, chemical flush inner tank, and install fresh magnesium anode rod.

1. Gas Geyser — Hydro-Mechanical Combustion Architecture

LPG gas water heaters operate without 220V electric connection, utilizing two 1.5V D-cell batteries for electronic pulse ignition. When a hot water tap is opened, water flow lifts an internal diaphragm governor that trips a microswitch, triggering electronic sparks and energizing the electromagnetic gas solenoid valve to ignite the multi-jet copper heat exchanger burner.

2. Common Gas Geyser Faults & Diagnostic Solutions

Fault: Sparking tick-tick sound present but main burner flame does not ignite

Root Cause: Gas solenoid valve seized, low gas cylinder pressure, or blocked burner pilot jets.

Diagnostic Solution: Test solenoid magnetic coil resistance, clear carbon deposits from burner nozzles, and test regulator pressure.

Fault: Geyser flame ignites for 5–10 seconds and turns off automatically

Root Cause: Flame ionization sensor thermocouple rod coated with carbon soot.

Diagnostic Solution: Clean thermocouple sensor tip with fine emery cloth, check flame contact position, and test safety shut-off timing.

Common Geyser Problems We Handle

Our visiting technicians frequently diagnose and resolve these 30 common geyser faults across Roorkee homes:

1. Geyser Not Heating Water
What You Notice: The power switch is turned on and indicator lights may glow, but water stays completely cold.
Possible Reasons: Burned out electric heating coil, broken internal wiring, or a blown heating element fuse.
What Technician Checks: The technician tests electrical supply voltage at the terminal block and checks heating element continuity with a multimeter.
Possible Repair / Part Involved: Replacement of the faulty heating element and tightening of electrical terminals.
2. Water Heating Very Slowly
What You Notice: Water takes 40 to 60 minutes to become lukewarm instead of the usual 10 to 15 minutes.
Possible Reasons: Heavy calcium and mineral scale encrusting the heating element, reducing heat transfer into water.
What Technician Checks: The technician drains the tank, inspects the element surface for hard water encrustation, and tests element resistance.
Possible Repair / Part Involved: Chemical descaling of the heating element or replacement if the copper sheath is damaged.
3. Geyser Heating Too Much (Overheating)
What You Notice: Scalding boiling water or steam hissing out of hot water taps accompanied by vibration.
Possible Reasons: Primary thermostat contacts are fused together in the closed position, failing to cut off power at the set temperature.
What Technician Checks: The technician tests thermostat cutoff temperature calibration using a digital thermometer.
Possible Repair / Part Involved: Immediate replacement of the faulty primary stem thermostat and testing of the secondary safety cutout.
4. No Hot Water Coming Out
What You Notice: Geyser turns on normally, but hot water taps deliver zero water or only an empty air hiss.
Possible Reasons: Air lock in the pipeline, clogged hot water outlet nipple, or blocked non-return safety valve.
What Technician Checks: The technician inspects the cold inlet valve flow, clears pipe airlocks, and checks outlet pipe clearance.
Possible Repair / Part Involved: Flushing sediment from the inlet/outlet connection pipes or replacing choked safety valves.
5. Geyser Not Switching ON
What You Notice: Neither the red nor green indicator light turns on when the main wall switch is pressed.
Possible Reasons: Blown power socket fuse, loose power cord connection, or the secondary thermal reset switch has tripped.
What Technician Checks: The technician tests the 16A power socket voltage, cord continuity, and checks the manual reset button.
Possible Repair / Part Involved: Resetting the thermal cutout switch, replacing the power cord, or fixing internal terminal wiring.
6. Geyser Switching OFF Repeatedly
What You Notice: The geyser heats for 2 minutes and shuts off automatically before water is sufficiently hot.
Possible Reasons: Thermostat calibrated incorrectly, thermal cutout tripping prematurely due to scale heat accumulation.
What Technician Checks: The technician tests thermostat cycle duration and inspects the temperature sensing pocket inside the tank.
Possible Repair / Part Involved: Cleaning scale from the thermostat sensor pocket and replacing worn thermostat assemblies.
7. Pilot Light Not Working in Gas Geyser
What You Notice: Opening the hot water tap produces no pilot flame or igniter clicking sound in gas geysers.
Possible Reasons: Exhausted 1.5V D-size igniter batteries, oxidized battery box contacts, or a damaged pulse ignition unit.
What Technician Checks: The technician tests battery voltage under load and inspects the battery compartment wiring.
Possible Repair / Part Involved: Cleaning battery contacts, replacing ignition batteries, or installing a new electronic pulse igniter.
8. Gas Geyser Not Igniting
What You Notice: The igniter clicks continuously when water is turned on, but burner flames do not light.
Possible Reasons: Gas cylinder regulator turned off, gas supply hose kinked, or gas solenoid valve coil burned out.
What Technician Checks: The technician checks gas pressure line connections and tests the electromagnetic gas solenoid valve.
Possible Repair / Part Involved: Clearing the burner gas line, adjusting gas pressure, or replacing the gas solenoid valve.
9. Water Leakage from Geyser Body
What You Notice: Water droplets or steady trickling seen coming from the bottom plastic cover of the geyser.
Possible Reasons: Worn flange rubber gasket, loose element mounting bolts, or pinhole rust leak in the inner tank.
What Technician Checks: The technician removes the lower cover to isolate whether water leaks from the flange seal or inner tank weld.
Possible Repair / Part Involved: Replacing the element flange gasket and tightening bolts. If the inner tank is rusted through, tank replacement is advised.
10. Leakage from Inner Tank Area
What You Notice: Continuous water seepage through outer casing seam joints, soaking bathroom walls.
Possible Reasons: Electrochemical galvanic corrosion in older stainless steel or glass-lined tanks without an active anode.
What Technician Checks: The technician inspects the internal tank weld seams with the outer insulation removed.
Possible Repair / Part Involved: Inner tank replacement or advice on upgrading to an anti-corrosion glass-coated water heater.
11. Leakage from Inlet or Outlet Pipe Connections
What You Notice: Water spraying or dripping at the threaded connection between geyser nipples and flexible braided hoses.
Possible Reasons: Perished rubber washer inside the connection hose, loose threading, or cracked brass nipple.
What Technician Checks: The technician inspects hose threads, washer condition, and brass nipple integrity.
Possible Repair / Part Involved: Replacing flexible connection braided pipes, installing fresh Teflon tape, and new heavy-duty rubber washers.
12. Leakage Around Heating Element Flange
What You Notice: Water pooling around electrical terminals inside the bottom enclosure, posing electrical danger.
Possible Reasons: Hard water minerals have degraded the rubber O-ring or triangular flange gasket seal.
What Technician Checks: The technician inspects the flange plate for corrosion warping and tests gasket elasticity.
Possible Repair / Part Involved: Replacing the heating element flange gasket seal and checking electrical terminals for corrosion.
13. Tripping MCB or House Circuit Breaker
What You Notice: Turning on the geyser instantly trips the main distribution board MCB or ELCB/RCCB switch.
Possible Reasons: Electric heating element sheath has cracked, allowing live 230V current to short into the water tank (earth fault).
What Technician Checks: The technician uses an insulation resistance tester (megger) to measure element resistance to ground.
Possible Repair / Part Involved: Immediate replacement of the grounded heating element and verification of proper earthing wire safety.
14. Burning Smell from Geyser
What You Notice: A sharp burning plastic or wire smell inside the bathroom while the geyser is running.
Possible Reasons: Loose screw at terminal block causing high contact resistance, overheating, and wire insulation melting.
What Technician Checks: The technician opens the electrical cover to inspect terminal blocks, wiring harness, and thermostat lugs.
Possible Repair / Part Involved: Trimming damaged wires, installing new heat-resistant ceramic connectors, and securing tight connections.
15. Unusual Electrical Sparking or Humming Sound
What You Notice: Crackling sounds near the geyser switch or continuous buzzing sound inside the geyser base.
Possible Reasons: Loose wire terminal sparking under high 2000W current load or a vibrating electromagnetic contactor.
What Technician Checks: The technician checks terminal screws for carbon deposition and measures electrical current draw with a clamp meter.
Possible Repair / Part Involved: Cleaning carbonized terminal points, replacing burnt wire lugs, and ensuring secure wire clamps.
16. Clicking Sound Without Ignition (Gas Geyser)
What You Notice: Fast clicking sound occurs when tap opens, but no flame ignites and clicking stops after 10 seconds.
Possible Reasons: Burner nozzle choked with dust, low water flow pressure unable to fully push the gas diaphragm.
What Technician Checks: The technician tests water flow rate through the venturi valve and inspects burner jet orifices.
Possible Repair / Part Involved: Servicing the water diaphragm assembly, cleaning gas burner jets, and setting electrode spark gap.
17. Geyser Making Loud Popping or Boiling Noise
What You Notice: Kettle-like whistling, knocking, or popping rumbling sounds coming from the tank while heating.
Possible Reasons: Thick layer of mineral scale traps boiling steam bubbles underneath, causing localized cavitation.
What Technician Checks: The technician inspects tank bottom sediment and checks heating element scale encrustation.
Possible Repair / Part Involved: Draining the water heater, flushing sediment scale, and descaling or replacing the heating element.
18. Water Pressure Problem (Low Hot Water Flow)
What You Notice: Cold water tap has strong flow, but hot water tap trickles very slowly from the geyser.
Possible Reasons: Inlet non-return safety valve choked with lime scale or outlet pipe clogged with mineral flakes.
What Technician Checks: The technician tests pressure before and after the safety valve and inspects connection hose mesh.
Possible Repair / Part Involved: Descaling or replacing the multi-function safety valve and flushing hot water outlet lines.
19. Thermostat Not Cutting Off
What You Notice: Geyser indicator stays in heating mode continuously without ever turning off.
Possible Reasons: Bimetallic strip inside the thermostat stem has lost elasticity or contact points have fused.
What Technician Checks: The technician tests cutoff temperature in water using a digital thermometer probe.
Possible Repair / Part Involved: Replacing the faulty primary thermostat with an exact factory-specified temperature stem.
20. Heating Element Burned Out or Scaled
What You Notice: Zero heating or lukewarm water accompanied by high electricity consumption.
Possible Reasons: Dry heating without water in the tank, power surges, or heavy lime scale blocking heat dissipation.
What Technician Checks: The technician tests element resistance with a multimeter (standard 2kW element should read ~26 ohms).
Possible Repair / Part Involved: Replacing the blown heating element with a heavy-duty copper or enamel-coated replacement element.
21. Temperature Control Knob Loose or Unresponsive
What You Notice: Turning the external dial from Low to High does not change the water temperature.
Possible Reasons: Broken plastic spindle coupling connecting the external dial to the internal thermostat shaft.
What Technician Checks: The technician checks the internal shaft engagement and tests mechanical knob movement.
Possible Repair / Part Involved: Replacing the rotary control knob coupling or replacing the capillary adjustable thermostat.
22. Indicator Light (Red / Green) Not Working
What You Notice: Geyser heats water properly, but the power or heating status indicator lamp remains dark.
Possible Reasons: Burnt neon bulb, broken resistor lead, or disconnected spade connector on the indicator harness.
What Technician Checks: The technician tests voltage across indicator lamp terminals.
Possible Repair / Part Involved: Replacing the LED / neon indicator lamp assembly and securing terminal connections.
23. Digital Display or Smart PCB Control Fault
What You Notice: Smart geyser LED screen shows blank display, error codes, or touch buttons do not register.
Possible Reasons: Voltage fluctuation damage to the internal power supply module or moisture ingress on PCB traces.
What Technician Checks: The technician tests DC voltage outputs from the transformer and checks PCB capacitor health.
Possible Repair / Part Involved: Repairing or replacing the electronic control board and sealing the display panel against moisture.
24. Water Taking Too Long to Heat
What You Notice: Heating duration has progressively increased over months from 15 minutes to over an hour.
Possible Reasons: Severely scaled heating element, partial element coil burnout, or degraded tank thermal insulation.
What Technician Checks: The technician measures current draw during operation and inspects element scale thickness.
Possible Repair / Part Involved: Complete tank descaling, flushing lime sediment, and installing a high-efficiency replacement element.
25. Geyser Shutting Down During Hot Shower
What You Notice: Geyser suddenly loses power mid-shower and cannot be restarted immediately.
Possible Reasons: Secondary thermal cutout switch has tripped due to internal steam buildup or inadequate water pressure.
What Technician Checks: The technician checks the manual reset button, water inlet pressure, and thermostat calibration.
Possible Repair / Part Involved: Diagnosing the root cause of overheating, resetting the safety switch, and replacing faulty thermostats.
26. Rusty or Brownish Discolored Hot Water
What You Notice: Hot water comes out with a red-brown tint or metallic odor, while cold water is clear.
Possible Reasons: The sacrificial magnesium anode rod has completely dissolved, allowing water to oxidize inner tank steel.
What Technician Checks: The technician removes the anode rod for visual inspection of remaining magnesium volume.
Possible Repair / Part Involved: Flushing rust particles from the tank and installing a new sacrificial magnesium anode rod.
27. Safety Valve Dripping or Leaking Continuously
What You Notice: Continuous stream of water draining from the safety valve discharge spout even when not heating.
Possible Reasons: Incoming municipal water pressure exceeds valve limit (8 bar), or grit is trapped on valve seat.
What Technician Checks: The technician tests water inlet pressure and manually actuates the safety valve lever to flush debris.
Possible Repair / Part Involved: Cleaning the valve seat or replacing the multi-function safety valve (MFSV). Adding a pressure-reducing valve if line pressure is too high.
28. High Water Expansion or Pressure Buildup
What You Notice: Flexible connection pipes bulge or leak under high expansion pressure during heating cycles.
Possible Reasons: Safety relief valve is jammed shut with lime scale, preventing normal thermal expansion release.
What Technician Checks: The technician tests safety valve spring relief action using manual test lever.
Possible Repair / Part Involved: Immediate replacement of the seized safety valve to ensure tank structural safety.
29. Gas Smell or Incomplete Combustion Concern
What You Notice: Odor of unburned LPG gas or soot accumulation around the gas geyser exhaust vent.
Possible Reasons: Gas regulator leak, cracked gas hose, clogged burner aeration holes, or poor bathroom ventilation.
What Technician Checks: The technician performs soap bubble leak testing on all gas joints and inspects burner flame color (should be clear blue, not yellow).
Possible Repair / Part Involved: Replacing gas connection pipe, cleaning burner aeration slots, adjusting air-gas mixture, and ensuring safe flue exhaust.
30. Loose Wall Mounting or Heavy Vibration
What You Notice: Geyser unit wobbles on the wall or vibrates with a hum when water flow starts.
Possible Reasons: Wall anchor bolts have loosened in soft plaster, or bracket is improperly seated.
What Technician Checks: The technician inspects wall anchor fastener grip and verifies bracket leveling.
Possible Repair / Part Involved: Re-fastening with heavy-duty metal anchor bolts and re-mounting the unit securely on solid brick/concrete.
Important Safety Notice:

Water heaters combine high 230V electric current (or combustible LPG gas) with water pressure. If you notice a burning smell, gas odor, electrical sparks, or continuous MCB tripping, turn off the appliance immediately and discontinue use. Never open high-voltage electrical covers or gas burner pipes yourself. Seek professional technician inspection.

A thermostat automatically regulates water temperature. It switches off power when water reaches the set temperature (typically 65°C) and turns it back on when water cools down, preventing overheating and saving electricity.
A multi-function safety valve (MFSV) protects the geyser tank from excessive water pressure, prevents hot water from back-flowing into the cold water supply line, and releases thermal expansion pressure during heating.
