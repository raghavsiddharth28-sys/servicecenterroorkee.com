1. Solo Microwave — Core Architecture

Solo microwaves utilize a 2.45 GHz magnetron tube powered by a high-voltage transformer, high-voltage capacitor (approx. 0.95uF–1.05uF), and high-voltage diode to generate electromagnetic radio-frequency waves directly into the cavity. Capacities range from 17 to 23 litres with mechanical dial timers or digital membrane touchpads.

2. Common Solo Faults & Diagnostic Solutions

Fault: Microwave runs, timer counts down, but food stays completely cold

Root Cause: Shorted high-voltage diode, open high-voltage capacitor, or exhausted magnetron filament.

Diagnostic Solution: Discharge capacitor safely with 20k ohm resistor probe, measure diode conduction under 9V battery bias, test filament continuity (<1 ohm), and replace defective component.

Fault: Loud buzzing noise and bright sparking near right-hand cavity wall

Root Cause: Food grease splatter carbonized on mica waveguide cover creating electrical arc path to antenna.

Diagnostic Solution: Clean and degrease waveguide aperture, inspect magnetron antenna dome for burn pits, and fit brand new heat-resistant mica waveguide plate.

Fault: Turntable glass tray not rotating or making clicking noise

Root Cause: Broken plastic clover center coupler or stripped synchronous motor internal gear train.

Diagnostic Solution: Inspect underside plastic coupler, measure motor winding resistance (2.5k–3.5k ohms), and install replacement 220V/240V synchronous motor.

1. Convection Microwave — True 3D Baking Mechanism

Convection ovens feature a rear circular heating coil (1400W–2000W) and an electric blower fan that circulates forced hot air evenly throughout the stainless steel cavity. This enables baking temperatures from 110°C to 220°C, mimicking traditional baker ovens while retaining microwave speed.

2. Common Convection Faults & Diagnostic Solutions

Fault: Cakes fail to rise or cook evenly; oven cannot reach 180°C preheat

Root Cause: Rear circular convection heater element burned out or rear convection fan motor stopped.

Diagnostic Solution: Test circular element ohms (approx. 25–35 ohms), inspect rear motor winding, and replace failed heating element or blower fan assembly.

Fault: Loud rattling sound and vibration from back cabinet in baking mode

Root Cause: Convection fan motor bronze bushing worn out or warped fan blade striking air baffle.

Diagnostic Solution: Disassemble rear shroud, center and balance fan blade, lubricate with high-temp synthetic grease or install replacement blower motor.

Fault: Temperature sensor error code (Err2 / E-24 / E-32) during preheat

Root Cause: NTC cavity thermistor sensor disconnected, scaled with carbon, or open circuit.

Diagnostic Solution: Access rear sensor probe, measure resistance at room temperature, and install new calibrated NTC thermistor.

Below are the most frequent genuine microwave failures reported across Roorkee residences. For each fault, our technicians follow a systematic diagnostic methodology:

1. Microwave Not Turning ON
Possible Reason: Fused main input ceramic fuse (15A/20A), tripped thermal protector on cavity, or damaged supply cord.
What Technician Checks: AC mains continuity, 230V socket voltage, line filter board, and thermal thermostat switches.
Possible Solution: Ceramic safety fuse replacement, power cord renewal, or thermal cutoff replacement.

Possible Reason: Defective magnetron tube, shorted high-voltage diode, open HV fuse, or weak capacitor.

Possible Solution: Magnetron tube replacement, HV diode replacement, or HV capacitor renewal.

Crucial Microwave Safety Information

A microwave oven is NOT like other small home appliances. Never attempt DIY repair or remove the metal outer cabinet cover yourself.

  • • Lethal Voltage Stored: The high-voltage capacitor stores over 2,000 to 4,000 Volts DC even when the microwave is completely unplugged from the wall. Touching internal terminals can cause severe electric shock or death.
  • • Radiation Hazard: Operating a microwave with a bent door, broken glass, or bypassed door microswitches can leak hazardous microwave radiation into your kitchen.
  • • Beryllium Oxide Risk: The ceramic insulator on magnetron tubes can release hazardous toxic dust if cracked or physically broken during amateur handling.
  • • Immediate Action on Sparking: If your microwave starts sparking, smoking, or emitting an acrid burning odor, immediately switch off the wall power switch, unplug the unit, and call a trained technician.
